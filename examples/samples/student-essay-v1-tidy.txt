Was the printing press the most important invention?

I think the printing press changed more than people realise, but I am not sure it beats everything else. My history teacher said it "broke the monopoly on knowledge" and that line stuck with me, so this essay is basically me arguing with it.

Before Gutenberg, books were copied by hand, which meant a book cost about as much as a small house. Only the church and rich families owned them. After the press, the same book could be made in hundreds of copies, and the price fell. That part is clear. What I find more interesting is what people did with the cheap books. They argued. The Reformation happened partly because Luther's ideas could be printed faster than the church could answer them. Actually that is too simple - Luther was not the first person to complain about the church, he was just the first one who could be read everywhere at once.

The counter argument is obvious though. If nobody can read, a cheap book is still useless. Literacy in Europe was maybe 10 percent when the press arrived, so the effect took two hundred years to really show. So maybe the important invention was schools, not printing.

I also think we overrate old inventions because we know how the story ends. The people living in 1500 did not walk around saying "the information age has begun". They were worried about bread and plague.

My conclusion is that the printing press was necessary but not sufficient. It made mass literacy worth having, and then literacy made the press worth using. Neither one alone does much. If I had to pick one invention I would probably still say writing itself, which is a boring answer but I think it is the honest one.
