Was the printing press the most important invention?

Honestly I didnt want to write about this. Everyone picks the printing press. But my dad works in a print shop and he laughed when I told him the topic, so here we are.

Before Gutenberg a book was copied by hand. One book, one person, months of work. My teacher said a Bible cost about as much as a house and I didnt believe her until I looked it up. So obviously only churches and rich people had them.

Then the press arrives and suddenly you can make hundreds. Prices drop. More people own books. That bit everyone agrees on.

The part I actually care about is what happened next, which is that people started arguing with each other in print. Luther is the famous example — his complaints about the church spread faster than the church could reply. Wait, thats not quite right. Other people had complained before him, loads of them. The difference was that Luther could be read in twenty cities in a month instead of one monastery in a year.

But heres the problem with my own argument. If you cant read, a cheap book does nothing for you. Literacy was something like ten percent. Ten! So the press sat there being amazing for about two centuries before most people could use it.

So which invention was it really, the press or the schools?

I think you need both and thats a bit of a cop out but its what I actually believe. The press made learning to read worth the effort. Reading made the press worth building. If someone forced me to pick one thing Id say writing, which I know is a boring answer, and my teacher will probably write "expand this" in the margin
